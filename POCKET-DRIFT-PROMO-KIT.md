Pocket Drift: Overdrive starts with a familiar object and changes its scale: a workshop bench becomes the setting for a miniature motorsport world. Painted curbs and a sweeping circuit sit among objects that would usually belong beside a toolbox. The contrast is the idea's appeal: little cars with the presentation of a much larger race.

The current garage includes Scrapper, Volt Dozer, and Neon Viper, each with its own color and silhouette. They are original stylized designs; the artwork should not be read as a licensed vehicle lineup. Scene assembly and rendering have been checked in Unity, but that is only one part of making a racer.

As the creator, my next priority is the experience behind those images. The drift and combat-racing direction needs hands-on validation, and I want to test race completion, collisions, recovery, and performance before calling a build ready for wider testing. This is an early look at the project, with more progress to share as those pieces become reliable.

## Advertising and beta rollout

1. Start with a small private test rather than paid ads. Verify a packaged build launches on a second PC, completes races, restarts reliably, and has working controls and recovery. Include version, controls, known issues, minimum tested hardware, and feedback instructions.
2. Create a restricted itch.io page for the first invited testers. Its official access controls support restricted visibility. Keep private download credentials off public pages. Publish a public landing page separately when the presentation is ready: https://itch.io/docs/creators/access-control and https://itch.io/docs/creators/limited-releases.
3. Use one public website URL in profiles. Initially point visitors at updates; once ready, replace the unavailable status with a real signup destination. A form should explain the test, eligibility, how contact details will be used, and how to withdraw. Ask only for email, platform, and optional hardware. Keep marketing consent separate from test coordination.
4. For each test, ask players to complete one race and report steering clarity, recovery issues, frame-rate problems, and their biggest frustration. Collect build version and reproduction steps. Ask permission separately before publishing quotes or player footage.
5. Publish three useful posts per week: a short actual driving clip when available, a before/after development change, and a question about one specific design choice. Cross-edit for vertical video; label renders. Track visits, signups, testers who launch, completed sessions, and actionable reports rather than just views.
6. Check each Reddit community's current self-promotion rules before posting. Lead with development detail and an answerable question. Disclose that you are the creator. Don't duplicate the same promotional message across communities or seed fake reviews.
7. Prepare Steam after the core loop and honest gameplay footage are ready. Steam Direct currently lists a $100 fee per product and a 30-day wait after payment before release. Its onboarding also requires a public Coming Soon period. Steam Playtest is a route for controlled testing through your store presence. Review the current official requirements before paying or scheduling: https://partner.steamgames.com/steamdirect/ and https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/features/playtest.
8. Explore smaller communities as discovery channels, not guaranteed distribution: IndieDB for development coverage, TIGSource for development discussion, and Game Jolt if its current publishing options fit the project. Check current activity, submission rules, and audience fit before investing setup time. Reuse a compact press kit: description, accurate screenshots, labeled footage, creator contact, and one canonical URL.
9. Before public store submissions, verify licenses and ownership for music, fonts, art, and code; accurately complete each platform's content and AI-use disclosures based on the actual production process. Don't claim mobile support, multiplayer, finished weapons, or release timing until verified.
10. After testers can reliably play and feedback shows a clear hook, consider a small capped advertising experiment. Compare two actual gameplay hooks leading to the same landing page. Set the spend cap and success criterion before launch; pause if clicks don't become meaningful test participation.

## First four weeks / proposed sequence, not release promises

- Week 1: Validate the core race loop, capture honest footage, prepare private test instructions.
- Week 2: Invite a small first group through restricted access; collect bugs and usability feedback. Post one visual progress update.
- Week 3: Fix the largest repeated issues, retest, publish a before/after devlog and a short creator-preview video.
- Week 4: If the build is reliable, open a limited signup round and begin storefront preparation. If it is not, keep testing privately and explain the next milestone.
